Growth & remodeling literature: what was modelled, how, and how it relates to our code

A summary of the 13 papers in .context/Papers (plus the Drews and Weiss supplements). For each paper: what was modelled, which constrained-mixture variant, constituents, production and removal laws, the growth kinematics, the FE method (code, elements, integration), the dimension and geometry, fluid coupling, the solver and how convergence was ensured, and how convincing the results are. After that come a cross-paper comparison, the relation to our JAX code, and the effort needed to replicate each paper in it.

Notation: FCMM / CMM = full (heredity-integral) constrained mixture; HCMM = homogenized CMM (Cyron 2016); CMMe = mechanobiologically equilibrated CMM (Latorre 2018/2020); \(\Upsilon\) = stimulus function; \(K_\sigma, K_\tau\) = gains for intramural and wall shear stress; WSS = wall shear stress. "Not stated" means the paper does not say.

Humphrey & Rajagopal 2002: A constrained mixture model for growth and remodeling of soft tissues

Math. Models Methods Appl. Sci. 12 (2002). The foundation of all the other papers.

What
A conceptual and theoretical framework, with no numerical study. Each constituent has its own natural configuration that evolves through continuous production and removal. New mass is deposited with a preferred (deposition) stretch, and all constituents are constrained to move with the mixture.
Key ideas
Rule of mixtures for stress and strain energy. Mass balance with separate production and removal (survival functions). Deposition stretch as a constitutive quantity. Homeostasis as the target state. Analogy with evolving natural configurations in plasticity.
Constituents
Generic; elastin, collagen and smooth muscle as the motivating example.
Production / removal
Postulated as general functions of stress and other stimuli; no specific law.
Numerics / geometry / fluid
None.
Assessment
foundational No results to judge. Our FCMM implements this concept at a material point.

Cyron, Aydin & Humphrey 2016: A homogenized constrained mixture (and mechanical analog) model

Biomech. Model. Mechanobiol. 15 (2016). The origin of the HCMM used in our code.

What
Replace the cohort history of each constituent by a single temporally homogenized inelastic deformation \(F_r^\alpha\), and growth by \(F_g\). The resulting rate law for stress, \(\dot\sigma = -(\dot\rho_+/\rho + 1/T)(\sigma-\sigma_\text{pre})\), is interpreted as a Maxwell fluid in parallel with a cell "motor element".
Constituents
Elastin (incompressible neo-Hookean), circumferential smooth muscle, 4 collagen families (0°, 90°, ±45°), all Fung.
Production / removal
\(\dot\varrho^i_0=\varrho^i_0\,k_\sigma(\sigma^i-\sigma^i_\text{pre})/\sigma^i_\text{pre}\), stress-mediated; turnover time \(T = 70\) d. 1D fibers remodel through a scalar inelastic stretch \(\lambda_r\) (closed form, Eq. 25).
Growth
Kinematic, \(F_g\) from the mass change.
Numerics
MATLAB. A uniaxial (1D) tissue-equivalent example, and an axisymmetric cylindrical membrane model of an AAA (from Cyron 2014) with a focal elastin loss. Explicit time stepping, \(\Delta s = T/7\).
Results
HCMM ≈ FCMM in the near-linear regime, with moderate deviations only for the fastest-growing aneurysms after about 10 years. Much cheaper than the FCMM. Stability (bounded vs unbounded enlargement) is governed by \(k_\sigma\).
Assessment
core method Sound; the comparison with the FCMM is convincing. Validation against tissue-equivalent data is only qualitative.
For us
Already implemented (hcmm.core). The fiber closed form \(\lambda_r\) matches our Fung F_r / flow_exp.

Maes & Famaey: How to implement constrained mixture G&R algorithms for soft biological tissues

J. Mech. Behav. Biomed. Mater. 140 (2023) 105733 (file named 2020). Our main implementation reference.

What
A step-by-step implementation of the FCMM and the HCMM in Abaqus UMATs, compared on models A–E under the load cases U (prescribed stretch), S (prescribed stress) and F (prescribed force), plus an arterial example.
Constituents
Elastin matrix (neo-Hookean, compressible or incompressible) plus collagen fiber families (Fung, tension-only).
Production / removal
Eq. 24: stress-driven production with gain \(k_\sigma\) and turnover time \(T\); removal with time constant \(T\) (models differ in what is stress-mediated).
Growth
\(F_g\) isotropic (Eq. 6) or anisotropic (Eq. 7, along \(e_r\)).
Numerics
Abaqus/Standard 2017 UMATs. Single C3D8 or C3D8H (hybrid, incompressible) elements; quarter cylinder with 1920 C3D8. Forward Euler for \(\rho\) and \(F_r\); stress with the old \(F_r\), then the update (same as our explicit HCMM). A step-size convergence study (\(T\) = 101 d, steps up to \(T\)).
Results
HCMM and FCMM agree well; HCMM is much faster. The step size matters, especially for the FCMM.
Assessment
reproduced Our maes_benchmark reproduces models A–E (HCMM vs FCMM 0.2–3 %) and Fig. 4 of the artery (λθ(1000 d) = 1.184). Their UMATs are in .context/FortranFiles.

Sempértegui & Avril 2023: Re-interpretation of the HCMM within the plasticity framework

The basis of our core_implicit.py.

What
Extends the HCMM to 3D (isotropic) constituents such as the ground matrix and SMCs. The backward-Euler version of the stress rate law gives a "backstress" \(\sigma_\text{back}\) and a yield-like function \(f=\tfrac12|\hat\tau'|^2=0\), solved with plasticity tools (exponential map, flow rule).
Constituents
Nearly incompressible neo-Hookean 3D constituents, dispersed fibers; examples with 2 materials and explicit coupling in the Jacobian.
Production / removal
Generic \(\dot\rho_{0+}, \dot\rho_{0-}\); examples with balanced turnover and cell-seeded collagen.
Numerics
In-house FEM, local Newton–Raphson on the remodeling unknowns, consistent tangent by perturbation (finite differences). Cubes with 2×2×2 elements (Dirichlet BCs, refinement study done).
Results
Demonstrations only (homeostasis maintained, stresses converging to the prestress, two-material coupling).
Assessment
useful idea, formulation degenerate We showed in this project that the exact \(f=0\) formulation is degenerate: singular Jacobian, 0/0 flow direction, drift in \(\det C_r\). The underlying backward-Euler target stress (Eq. 18) is the useful part. It is what core_implicit implements, with an exponential map, a deviatoric basis, growth coupled through \(F_g(\rho_\text{tot})\), and an implicit-function tangent by AD.

Latorre & Humphrey 2018: A mechanobiologically equilibrated constrained mixture model

Z. Angew. Math. Mech. 98 (2018).

What
For sustained stimuli and \(s \gg s_{G\&R}\), the heredity integrals of the FCMM can be pre-integrated exactly. The long-term state then follows from algebraic equations: balanced production and removal, \(\Upsilon_h = 1\), turnover constituents at their deposition stretch \(\hat\sigma^\alpha = G^\alpha \hat S(G^{\alpha2}) G^\alpha\), and elastin deformed elastically.
Constituents
Elastin (no turnover), smooth muscle (passive + active tone with vasoconstrictor ratio driven by WSS), collagen (4 families in general; 1 circumferential in the examples).
Production / removal
\(m^\alpha=k^\alpha\rho^\alpha\Upsilon^\alpha\), \(\Upsilon^\alpha = 1 + K^\alpha_\sigma\Delta\sigma - K^\alpha_\tau\Delta\tau_w\); removal \(k^\alpha = k^\alpha_o(1+\Delta\sigma^2)\). Stimulus: \(\tilde\sigma = \mathrm{tr}\,\sigma\) (mixture level), WSS \(\tau_w = 4\mu Q/\pi a^3\).
Numerics / geometry
Thin-walled cylinder, 4 algebraic equations (radius, thickness, collagen density, axial force), Newton–Raphson with the ideal-adaptation initial guess.
Results
The same end state as the full model (P, Q, λz = 1.5×). Full model: seconds; CMMe: hundredths of a second. Shows that WSS feedback is needed for mechano-adaptation (stable vs unbounded growth).
Assessment
solid Exact within its assumptions. It cannot capture transients or dynamic instabilities, by construction.
For us
A cheap, exact long-time check for our HCMM. For sustained loads, our time integration should approach this equilibrium.

Latorre & Humphrey 2020: Fast, rate-independent FE implementation of a 3D constrained mixture model

CMAME 368 (2020) 113156.

What
CMMe in 3D FE with finite rotations. The stress depends only on \(F_h\): non-turnover constituents \(\sigma^\zeta = \phi^\zeta F_h G^\zeta \hat S G^\zeta F_h^T\), turnover constituents \(\sigma^\xi=\phi^\xi_h R_h\hat\sigma^\xi_N R_h^T\) (constant rotated stress), mass fractions \(\phi(J_h)\) from implicit equations. The multiplier \(p_h\) comes from \(\Upsilon_h=1\) (e.g. \(\sigma_{vh}=\sigma_{vo}\)), not from incompressibility.
Constituents
Elastin (neo-Hookean, no turnover), circumferential SMC and 4 collagen families (Fung); mouse descending thoracic aorta parameters.
Production / removal
Through \(\Upsilon=1+K_\sigma\Delta\sigma_I - K_\tau\Delta\tau_w\) with \(\sigma_I=\mathrm{tr}\,\sigma\); \(K_\tau/K_\sigma\) as the main parameter. Insults: elastin damage \(\varphi^e\) (spatially Gaussian-like).
Growth
Implicit through \(J_h\) (mass), no separate \(F_g\).
Numerics
FEBio plugin; FEBio 2.8.2 was modified for non-symmetric tangents (minor symmetry only). Exact consistent linearization (derived by hand, Box II), so continuum and algorithmic tangents coincide. Two stages: (I) hyperelastic pre-load with penalty \(U(\ln J J_G)\), \(\kappa = 10^3 c_e\), storing \(\sigma_{vo}, \hat\sigma^\xi_N, F_o\); (II) G&R load steps. Linear displacement-based hex elements, 4 through the thickness (from a convergence study), up to 4×160×60 = 38 400 elements. No mixed formulation, because the G&R stage is not isochoric (\(J_h\) is free), so no volumetric locking is expected.
Convergence
Quadratic Newton without line search (Tables 2, 3); 10 load steps; 6.6 s (axisymmetric) and 11 min 20 s (38 400 elements) on one core. Divergence only near mechanobiological instabilities or at excessive mesh distortion.
Geometry / fluid
Mouse aorta cylinder; axisymmetric and asymmetric fusiform aneurysms; WSS from local Poiseuille using the local radius (no CFD).
Results
FE = semi-analytical solution for P, Q and λz changes and uniform elastin loss. Aneurysm shape depends strongly on the end BCs (fixed displacement vs force) and on WSS regulation at the healthy ends. Reorienting diagonal collagen gives axial growth and tortuosity.
Assessment
strong numerics equilibrium only Fast and well verified, but no transients (e.g. no step-pressure dynamics, no stenosis that reverses).

Weiss et al. 2020: Mechanics-driven mechanobiological mechanisms of arterial tortuosity

Science Advances 6 (2020) eabd3574, plus supplement.

What
Experiments plus simulation. Ex vivo descending thoracic aortas of wild-type and fibulin-5-null mice (elastic-fiber defect) were tested biaxially and imaged in 3D with pDIC. Regional stiffness was identified by an inverse virtual-fields method with FEBio. The tortuosity index (TI) was correlated with mechanical metrics, and G&R simulations tested the hypotheses.
Model
CMMe (Latorre 2020): elastin neo-Hookean, collagen and passive SMC Fung, no active tone. Insult: ~20 % loss of \(c_e\), optionally with diagonal collagen reoriented by ~30 %, applied as a smooth one-wave asymmetric material imperfection on a straight tube (or a geometric imperfection).
Numerics
Modified FEBio, 1×20×40 = 800 quadratic displacement-based hexahedra with full 3×3×3 Gauss integration, quasi-static load steps. Post-buckling-like G&R paths are followed through the imperfection.
Results
Elastic buckling alone does not explain persistent tortuosity. Tortuosity needs G&R (axial growth) in elastin-compromised vessels plus a perturbation (multiple hits). The normal aorta is robust against modest perturbations under isometric end conditions.
Assessment
well-founded A combined experimental and computational argument. Comparisons are qualitative or trend-level (TI correlations), with no quantitative prediction.
For us
Directly relevant to our buckling tools (arc_length, stability, PTC) and to carotid tortuosity and kinking.

Mousavi, Farzaneh & Avril 2019: Patient-specific predictions of ATAA growth with the HCMM

Biomech. Model. Mechanobiol. 18 (2019).

What
The first patient-specific HCMM FE model of an ascending thoracic aortic aneurysm (ATAA), driven by localized elastin proteolysis.
Constituents
Elastin (neo-Hookean with penalty \(\kappa(J^e_{el}-1)^2\), \(\kappa = 10\mu\) or \(100\mu\)), 4 collagen families (0°, 90°, ±45°), passive SMCs (Fung, with different parameters in tension and compression). Two layers: media (97 % of elastin, 100 % of SMCs, 15 % of axial and diagonal collagen) and adventitia (the rest, plus all circumferential collagen).
Production / removal
Collagen: \(\dot\varrho = \varrho\,k_\sigma(\sigma-\sigma_h)/\sigma_h + \dot D_g\). In the media driven by SMC stress, in the adventitia by collagen stress. Elastin only degrades: \(\dot D^e_g=-\varrho^e/T_e - (D_\max/t_\text{dam})\varrho^e(0)e^{-0.5(X_3/L_\text{dam})^2 - t/t_\text{dam}}\) (spatial and temporal damage). SMCs: no turnover, but continuous remodeling (\(1/T\) term).
Growth
Braeu 2017: common \(F_g\), rate \(\dot F_g=\sum(\dot\varrho^i/\varrho)[F_g^{-T}:a_g\otimes a_g]a_g\otimes a_g\), along the wall normal. Remodeling by Eq. 15 (2nd-PK form of the Cyron rate law).
Numerics
Abaqus UMAT; structured hexahedral mesh with edges along the radial, circumferential and axial directions; ATAA with 7700 hexahedra. Newton–Raphson per step; time step = 1 month. G&R explicit (from the stresses of the previous step). Prestress: iterative elastin deposition stretch (Mousavi & Avril 2017) at diastolic pressure.
Geometry / fluid
Thick cylinder (vs Braeu 2017), toric arch (R/r = 4), patient ATAA from CT (MIMICS segmentation, uniform thickness 2.38 mm). 4D-flow MRI and CFD were used only to place the elastin damage (WSS jet impingement); no coupling.
Results
Agrees with Braeu's cylinder. Stable or unstable dilatation depending on \(k_\sigma\). Patient ATAA: diameter 42 → 64 mm (small gain) or → 47 mm (stable). Stress shifts to the adventitia, and collagen is deposited mainly in the media.
Assessment
plausible, not validated No follow-up imaging comparison. Uniform thickness and material, and a prescribed damage law. The authors report instabilities when imposing arbitrary deposition stretches.

Zhang, Laubrie, Mousavi, Avril & Ben Ahmed 2023: Aneurysmal dilatation after chronic type B dissection

Patient-specific FE G&R of cTBAD.

What
G&R after an aortic dissection: the media and adventitia are two separate continua connected by stiff interfacial springs (\(k = 1000\) kPa/mm). The dissection is created by removing springs in a region, and the false-lumen (FL) pressure acts on the newly freed surfaces.
Constituents
Elastin, 4 collagen families (0°, 90°, ±45°), SMCs with active tone (λ0 = 0.8, λm = 1.4).
Production / removal
\(\dot\rho^i_R=\rho^i_R k^i_\sigma(\sigma^i-\sigma^i_h)/\sigma^i_h\) (stress-mediated only); remodeling of 1D fibers by the scalar law (Eq. 7, with \(T^i\)).
Growth
Braeu: common \(F_g\); prestretch \(F_r(t_0)=G_h^{-1}\); non-uniform prestretch for the patient case (Laubrie iterative method).
Numerics
Open-source Florence (Poya et al., Python/C++, high-order FE). Hexahedral meshes 6×40×25, 6×60×20, 6×48×42 (thickness × circumference × length). Three steps: homeostasis; opening; G&R increments, each solved by Newton–Raphson. About 2 h per simulation on a laptop.
Geometry / fluid
Cylinder, torus, patient descending-aorta segment from CT. Constant pressures in the true and false lumens; no CFD.
Results
Gain \(k_\sigma\) decides stable or excessive dilatation. A wide tear and a high FL pressure favour instability.
Assessment
mechanistically plausible, not validated No patient follow-up yet (stated as future work). The spring interface is simple and effective.

Schwarz et al. 2023: A fluid–solid–growth solver for cardiovascular modeling

CMAME 417 (2023) 116312.

What
Full 3D CMM (heredity integrals) coupled to 3D Navier–Stokes in svFSI (SimVascular). Long-term evolution of TEVGs and aortas with resolved WSS and pressure.
Constituents
Elastin (neo-Hookean), collagen families and passive or active SMCs (Fung, active tone driven by a WSS-dependent vasoconstrictor ratio), polymers (2 kinds, sigmoid degradation), ground matrix, and immuno-mediated constituents.
Production / removal
\(\Upsilon=1+K_\sigma\Delta\sigma_f-K_\tau\Delta\tau_f\), clipped at ≥ 0.1; inflammation \(\Upsilon=K\Gamma(\tau)/\max\Gamma\) with a gamma function. Removal \(k(t)=k_h(1+K_{D\sigma}\Delta\sigma+K_{D\tau}\Delta\tau)\). Stimulus: radially averaged \(\mathrm{tr}\,\sigma\) and WSS from the CFD. Minimum basal production for TEVGs.
Growth
Volume follows mass, \(J^{n+1}=\sum\rho^\alpha_R/\hat\rho^\alpha\), enforced by a dilatational penalty in a linearized (St Venant–Kirchhoff-like, prestressed) stress update for the solid–growth iteration.
Numerics
svFSI; solid with linear hexahedra (C3D8-type), generalized-α; fluid stabilized with RBVMS, generalized-α. Deformation-gradient histories at every Gauss point; trapezoidal rule for the heredity integrals; hemodynamic stimuli smoothed with a Laplacian kernel. Coupling: partitioned, steady (rigid-wall CFD → solid) or monolithic pulsatile FSI; solid–growth fixed point with Aitken relaxation. Tolerances \(10^{-3}\) on displacement, \(10^{-2}\) on other fields (loose).
Geometry
IVC validation (vs thin-walled reduced order), idealized TEVG between IVC segments, sheep TEVG from MRI, human aortic arch (healthy, 27 y) for 800 days, normal and hypertensive, and a pulsatile check.
Results
Excellent agreement with the reduced-order model. A stenosis changes the WSS proximally and distally (only visible with CFD). The TEVG becomes elliptic (qualitatively like the sheep data). The aorta thickens at low-WSS sites.
Assessment
most complete physics expensive, loose tolerances, not quantitatively validated The arch is not started from homeostasis, which the authors acknowledge. The tangent ignores the dependence of turnover on strain, so convergence is slow.

Pfaller et al. 2024: FSGe, a fast and strongly coupled 3D fluid–solid–growth interaction method

CMAME 431 (2024) 117259; code svFSIplus + svFSGe (GitHub).

What
CMMe (Latorre 2020) solid coupled strongly to 3D steady Navier–Stokes. WSS acts on the whole wall volume (radial propagation).
Constituents
Elastin, SMC, 4 collagen families (mouse aorta parameters from Latorre 2020). Insult: elastin stiffness loss up to 70 % and loss of mechanosensing \(K_{\tau\sigma}\to 0\) in an asymmetric patch.
Stimulus
\(\Delta\sigma_I = K_{\tau\sigma}\Delta\tau_w\) with \(\sigma_I = \mathrm{tr}\,\sigma/3\); WSS = magnitude of the CFD wall shear vector.
Numerics
svFSIplus. Fluid P1–P1 VMS; solid linear displacement-based hexahedra 64×1×40 (one element through the thickness, because more elements gave Gauss-point oscillations of the equilibria). Non-symmetric tangent → MUMPS direct solver (PETSc). Partitioned coupling: Gauss–Seidel for pre-load, then IQN-ILS (q = 20, filtering 0.1) with static relaxation (ω = 0.1) at the start, a linear predictor, and tolerance \(10^{-3}\). Weak coupling was unstable for \(K_{\tau\sigma}>0\); Aitken failed at high \(K_{\tau\sigma}\).
Cost
84 coupling iterations for \(K_{\tau\sigma} = 1\): fluid 52 min (6 cores), solid 1.7 min, mesh 2.7 min (M2 Max). The fluid is the bottleneck.
Results
FSGe ≈ solid-only G&R for \(K_{\tau\sigma}\to 0\). For \(K_{\tau\sigma}\to 1\) there are large differences: the healthy side grows inward with 3× thickness, and the fluid couples the aneurysm to the healthy wall. CMMe shows instabilities for \(K_{\tau\sigma}\to 1\).
Assessment
best FSG numerics idealized, no data Very transparent about failure modes (useful for us).

Drews et al. 2020: Spontaneous reversal of stenosis in tissue-engineered vascular grafts

Sci. Transl. Med. 12 (2020) eaax6919; supplement with model and code (.context/drews).

What
An FDA clinical trial of TEVGs (Fontan conduits in children) showed early stenosis, treated with angioplasty. A computational model predicted that the stenosis would reverse spontaneously (inflammation-driven, mechano-mediated), and this was then confirmed in a sheep IVC interposition model.
Constituents
Polymer (PGA, P(CL/LA): neo-Hookean, shear modulus from porosity, sigmoidal degradation \(Q_p\)), immuno-mediated and mechano-mediated collagen (4 families) and SMCs (Fung, with fiber maturation \(c(s,\tau)=c_h(1-e^{-k_c(s-\tau)})\)).
Production / removal
Inflammatory: \(m^i=m_h(1-e^{-\tau})K^i\Gamma(\tau)\) (gamma-shaped burden, gain depending on pore size). Mechano: \(m=m_h(1-e^{-\tau})(1+K_\sigma\Delta\sigma-K_\tau\Delta\tau_w)\) with \(\Delta\sigma=(r/r_h)/(h/h_h)-1\) and \(\Delta\tau_w=(r_h/r)^3-1\). Removal \(k(1+K_D)\), mechano part with \(K_D=(\lambda_{n(t)}/\lambda_{n(\tau)}-1)^2\).
Numerics / geometry
Full CMM, homogeneous axisymmetric thin-walled tube. Newton for the radius per step; RK2 explicit for the mass densities; time step reduced until converged (1 day). Compiled executable with a MATLAB driver (in the supplement).
Results
Parametric studies over 4 inflammatory parameters: dilatation, transient stenosis that reverses, or prolonged narrowing. Prediction confirmed in sheep (angiography, IVUS, histology).
Assessment
the strongest validation story A model-generated hypothesis tested prospectively in animals. Low-dimensional, but sufficient for the question.

Shirazi et al. 2025: Endogenous tissue restoration in biodegradable implants

Comput. Biol. Med. 196 (2025) 110741 (Famaey, Avril, Holzapfel; SimInSitu project).

What
An electrospun biodegradable conduit (Xeltis, UPy-polycarbonate) in sheep carotid arteries. The scaffold degrades, and neo-tissue (collagen + ground matrix) forms and remodels. Global sensitivity analysis, then calibration on 6-month data and prediction of 12-month data.
Constituents
Scaffold (anisotropic, Wollner-type invariants, degradation only), 4 collagen families (Fung), 3D ground matrix (neo-Hookean, 10 % of the collagen mass).
Production / removal
Scaffold: sigmoid degradation (as Drews). Collagen: \(\dot\rho_{0+}=\rho_h[1-e^{-t}](1+K_\sigma\Delta\sigma-K_\tau\Delta\tau)\), \(\dot\rho_{0-}=-k_h\rho(1+(I_4/G-1)^2)\). WSS stimulus from Poiseuille \((a_h/a)^3-1\).
Remodeling / growth
HCMM; collagen by the scalar \(\lambda_r\) rate law (Maes), ground matrix by the plasticity backward-Euler HCMM (Sempértegui); anisotropic growth along \(e_r\). They note that the 3D matrix stabilizes the numerics, because fibers alone cannot carry shear.
Numerics / geometry
Semi-analytical thick-walled axisymmetric cylinder in MATLAB, with an iterative solve for the deformed radius from the pressure balance per step. Mesh and time convergence: NRMSE < 0.05 with more than 2 points through the thickness and \(\Delta t\) < 1 day. GSA with SALib (Saltelli, 20 000 samples, 9 parameters).
Results
Inner diameter and neo-tissue density are predicted within the experimental standard deviation at 12 months. Scaffold degradation is overestimated. The scaffold stiffness and collagen production dominate the radius.
Assessment
calibration and prediction n = 3 + 3 animals, 1D model The VVUQ mindset (ASME V&V 40) is a good template. The carotid application is the closest to our project.

Prestress and initial homeostasis

  • Mousavi: iterative elastin deposition stretch (Mousavi & Avril 2017). Zhang: Laubrie iterative non-uniform prestretch.
  • Latorre: Stage I hyperelastic pre-load with a pre-Jacobian; stores \(\sigma_{vo}\) and \(\hat\sigma^\xi_N\), then computes \(F_h = F F_o^{-1}\).
  • Pfaller: thin-wall analytic homeostasis with freely chosen pressure (cannot use measured in-vivo pressures). Schwarz: the arch is not started from homeostasis.
  • Ours: Anderson-accelerated \(G_e\leftarrow F G_e\) until \(u = 0\) at the measured pressure (like Maes UMAT_DEP and Gebauer), differentiable through the prestress.

Relation to our project

What we have that none of the papers have
  • Adjoint gradients through prestress and the whole G&R history (verified against FD to 1e-9 … 1e-11). Shirazi needed 20 000 forward runs for a GSA and did calibration without gradients; we can calibrate with gradients.
  • Exact tangents by AD, including the implicit HCMM. Schwarz's convergence suffers from an approximate tangent; Latorre and Weiss derived theirs by hand; Sempértegui used finite differences.
  • Implicit (backward Euler) HCMM coupled to growth and explicit HCMM side by side; FCMM at the material point as a reference.
  • Hybrid elements with \(J_\text{target}=\det F_g\) for incompressible growing walls: Q1/P0 (hex8) and the inf-sup stable P2/P1 Taylor–Hood (tet10) with smooth, optimally converging pressures (MMS: u 2.97 / 1.91, pressure 2.5); verified incl. \(J_\text{target}\neq 1\) and free growth.
  • Fluid input as boundary conditions: wall pressure field BC.p and wall shear stress BC.wss per lumen point, mapped radially through the wall (as Pfaller), WSS stimulus \(-k_\tau\Delta\tau_w\) in production; adjoint gradients with respect to every wall pressure value. Demo: flow step reaches the Murray radius.
  • A verification suite (patch, MMS orders for all formulations, Lamé, Cook, consistency incl. adjoint, GCI: mesh order 2.00, time order 1.00). No paper reports MMS or GCI; Shirazi reports an NRMSE mesh and time check. Still missing: an independent code-to-code comparison (Abaqus).
  • Buckling tools (arc length, stability eigenvalues, PTC), relevant to Weiss-type tortuosity.
What the papers have that we lack
  • A fluid solver: the WSS stimulus and the fluid BCs exist, but the WSS must come from outside (Poiseuille stand-in or an external CFD). Schwarz and Pfaller solve 3D Navier–Stokes and couple strongly (Pfaller: IQN-ILS); we couple one-way per step. For carotid bifurcations a real CFD WSS is likely essential.
  • Intramural-stress driven thickening: with our fiber-stress driver the flow step dilates correctly but the wall thins (stress restored by remodeling, elastin carries the extra hoop load); Latorre's mixture-stress stimulus also restores the thickness.
  • CMMe: the cheapest route to long-term states; needed for Weiss / Pfaller-type studies.
  • Elastin degradation laws (Mousavi, Latorre) and active SMC tone (Latorre, Schwarz, Zhang).
  • Layer-specific walls (Mousavi, Zhang) and dissection (Zhang).
  • Inflammation and polymer constituents (Drews, Schwarz, Shirazi) for implants and TEVGs.
  • Experimental calibration and validation (Shirazi, Drews, Weiss).
Numerical lessons relevant to our FE choices
  • Pressure / G&R-variable oscillations: Pfaller saw Gauss-point oscillations of CMMe equilibria and used one element through the thickness; they propose a Q1P0-type field for G&R variables. Our own verification found pressure checkerboarding in P2/P0 (tet10) and inf-sup degradation of Q1/P0 in 2D-like problems; we implemented the stable pair P2/P1 (hybrid_p1), which removes the oscillation. The same idea (continuous lower-order field) would apply to G&R variables if CMMe shows Pfaller's Gauss-point oscillations.
  • Non-symmetric tangents: FEBio had to be modified; svFSIplus uses MUMPS. We already use non-symmetric direct solvers. Lesson from this session: always check the residual of the linear solve; PARDISO with perturbed pivots needs iterative refinement.
  • Locking: Latorre argues that CMMe does not lock, because \(J_h\) is free. For the HCMM with incompressible elastin our hybrid element is needed (Maes uses C3D8H likewise).
  • Coupling: for any fluid coupling, IQN-ILS with a predictor is the proven choice; weak coupling is unstable and Aitken is insufficient at high \(K_\tau\) (Pfaller).

Caveats

Adjoint gradients through an external CFD solver need that solver's adjoint (or a JAX-native CFD). Without that, FSG gradients would be partial (G&R part only). Patient-specific work needs segmented, meshed wall geometries with a radial direction; our Laplace wall basis already provides the latter.

Suggested order for the carotid project

  1. Latorre 2018 check (days): the long-time limit of our HCMM against the algebraic equilibrium. A cheap model verification.
  2. Poiseuille-type WSS stimulus done: BC.wss + k_tau, study flow_step (Murray radius reached).
  3. Elastin damage and SMC-mediated production (1–2 weeks): Cyron and Mousavi aneurysm cases; two-layer walls.
  4. Shirazi-type calibration on carotid data with our adjoint (2–4 weeks): the most direct route to a validated carotid model.
  5. CMMe (2–3 weeks): fast long-term states, Weiss tortuosity with our buckling tools.
  6. Fluid coupling (weeks to months): the BC side is ready (BC.p, BC.wss, face_points); connect an external steady CFD per step, later strong coupling with IQN-ILS (Pfaller's recipe).
